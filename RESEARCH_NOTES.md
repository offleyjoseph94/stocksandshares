## TSM — TSMC (ADR)

**What it does:** TSMC is the world's dominant independent semiconductor foundry — it manufactures chips designed by others (Nvidia, Apple, AMD, Broadcom, Qualcomm) at leading-edge process nodes. It does not design chips itself. The ADR (NYSE: TSM) represents 1 ordinary share. TSMC's node capability defines the frontier of AI computing — every major AI accelerator including Nvidia's Blackwell/Vera Rubin, Apple's M-series, and AMD's EPYC/Instinct runs through TSMC fabs.

**Latest results & financial health:** Q2 2026: revenue US$39B (+36% YoY), net income US$21.98B (+77.4% YoY) — the fifth consecutive quarter of record earnings and revenue. Gross margin consistently >53%. Capital expenditure guidance raised to TWD 60-64B (~$18.75-20B for 2026). Full-year 2026 revenue growth guidance lifted to "slightly above 40%" YoY in USD terms — up from prior ">30%" guide. Balance sheet: net cash positive, $20B+ liquid assets, debt well-covered by quarterly free cash flow of ~$10B+.

**Bull thesis:** TSMC operates a near-monopoly at the leading edge. At 3nm and below, only TSMC is ramping at volume — Samsung's competing 3nm suffered yield issues driving customers back to TSMC, and Intel Foundry's 18A node remains in qualification. N2 (2nm, Gate-All-Around) entered high-volume manufacturing in Q4 2025 at 90,000-100,000 wafers/month — roughly 4-5x Samsung's competing target. Every AI chip from every major fabless company runs through TSMC. Pricing power is evident: TSMC has raised foundry prices multiple times since 2022 with zero customer defection. The Arizona GigaFab ($165B committed: Fab 21 phases for N3, N2, and A16/1.6nm nodes) diversifies geopolitical risk and captures US CHIPS Act subsidies. TSMC is simultaneously the pick, the shovel, and the rail line of the AI era.

**Key catalysts ahead:** Monthly revenue disclosures (released each 10th of month) — a real-time AI demand gauge. Arizona Fab 21 Phase 2 (N3 production, 2027) and Phase 3 (N2/A16, 2028-2029) milestones. Q3 2026 earnings late October — first test of whether 40%+ FY2026 revenue growth is tracking. CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate) advanced packaging capacity expansion — CoWoS is the bottleneck bonding Nvidia GPU dies to HBM memory, and Nvidia's Blackwell supply is gated by CoWoS throughput.

**Main risks:** Taiwan/China geopolitical risk is the existential tail risk — a conflict or naval blockade would disrupt global AI chip supply in ways that cannot be quickly redirected. US-Taiwan tariff negotiations remain unresolved. Overseas fabs (Arizona, Kumamoto Japan, Dresden Germany) are structurally ~10-15% higher cost than Taiwan equivalents — as overseas production scales, margins may compress. Customer concentration: Nvidia alone is likely 15-20% of total revenue; Apple is probably another 15-20%.

**Competitive position:** Near-monopoly at 2nm-3nm. Samsung is the only other HVM competitor at these nodes but is 1-2 generations behind on yield and customer list. Intel Foundry is a potential long-term threat but is years behind. TSMC's CUDA-equivalent advantage is its customer ecosystem — every chip design tool, packaging technology, and IP library is optimised for TSMC processes.

**Rough valuation sense-check:** Market cap ~$2.1T at $404. 2026 revenue run-rate ~$156B at 40%+ growth → P/S ~13-14x. Forward P/E ~28-30x trailing — significantly cheaper than US chip design peers like Nvidia (40-50x). The gap is the "Taiwan geopolitical discount." Bull case: Arizona fabs de-risk the geopolitical overhang over 2027-2030, compressing the discount toward US-peer multiples.

## CRDO — Credo Technology Group

**What it does:** Credo makes active electrical cables (AECs), optical DSPs, SerDes/electro-optics IP, and now (via Dust Photonics acquisition) active optical components for AI data centers. In practical terms: every large Nvidia GPU cluster needs ultra-fast, low-power cable interconnects to link thousands of accelerators. Credo's AECs are the preferred choice for distances up to ~7 meters — cheaper than fiber, lower-power than traditional copper, and field-proven. The company also sells optical DSPs for longer runs and licences SerDes IP into networking chips.

**Latest results & financial health:** FY2026 (ended May 2026): revenue $1.34B (+205% YoY AEC expansion; revenue tripled overall). Non-GAAP EPS $2.51. Q4 FY2026: $437M revenue (+157% YoY). Q1 FY2027 guidance: $465-475M. Management outlook: >80% revenue growth in FY2027, supported by Dust Photonics acquisition (contributing $600M+ incremental revenue in FY2027). Five hyperscaler customers confirmed — three >10% revenue each in FY2026, a 5th customer won during the year. Analyst consensus PT raised to ~$151 from $110.

**Bull thesis:** AI data centres are fundamentally a cable problem at scale. A single 100,000-GPU cluster requires millions of interconnects; a 1M-GPU cluster (the direction of travel for hyperscalers) needs 10x that. Credo's AECs are the lowest-cost, lowest-power solution for the distances that dominate GPU cluster rack architecture. Five hyperscaler customers validates the technology with the most discerning and technically sophisticated buyers on the planet. The Dust Photonics acquisition moves Credo up the stack into active optical components — a $5B+ TAM where Broadcom currently dominates — creating a new growth vector and potential pricing power.

**Key catalysts ahead:** Q1 FY2027 earnings (August-September 2026) — management guided $465-475M with 80%+ FY2027 growth signalled; any beat or raise is a strong confirmation. 5th hyperscaler design-win ramping to revenue. Dust Photonics integration — whether the $600M incremental FY2027 revenue guide proves achievable. ZeroFlap AEC 2.0 (next-generation product) adoption and pricing.

**Main risks:** Extreme customer concentration — three hyperscalers represented >70% of FY2026 revenue; any deployment slowdown or supplier switch at one of these accounts would drop revenue sharply. High valuation at $207 (52-week range $61-$308): priced for continued hypergrowth; any miss re-rates harshly. Broadcom and Marvell are well-resourced competitors who could develop competing AEC/optical products. Dust Photonics integration risk — M&A adds complexity.

**Competitive position:** Market leader in AEC cables to hyperscalers; growing optical DSP share. First-mover advantage is real: data-centre operators standardise cable families across large deployments, making switching expensive once a vendor is qualified. Competes with Taiwan-based optical transceiver companies (InnoLight, Eoptolink) on optics price points.

**Rough valuation sense-check:** Market cap ~$11B at $207. FY2027 revenue likely $2.4B+ at 80% growth (including Dust Photonics) → P/S ~4.6x on forward revenue — actually modest for a company growing 80%+ YoY. The risk premium comes from hyperscaler-concentration rather than growth rate doubt.

## LEU — Centrus Energy

**What it does:** Centrus is the only US-licensed operator of a HALEU (High-Assay Low-Enriched Uranium) production facility. HALEU is the specialised nuclear fuel required by virtually every US-designed advanced reactor and SMR (small modular reactor) — including Oklo's Aurora, X-energy's Xe-100, TerraPower's Natrium, and BWX's BWRX-300. At its Piketon, Ohio facility, Centrus runs the only Western Hemisphere HALEU production cascade and is now scaling to commercial production under a DOE contract. The company also has a legacy LEU segment reselling enrichment services to conventional nuclear utilities.

**Latest results & financial health:** Q1 2026: revenue $76.7M; net income $10M (lower YoY due to delivery timing — the LEU segment's revenue is lumpy by contract). FY2026 revenue guidance raised to $450-500M (from prior guidance) after the DOE contract announcement in late June. Key event: June 30, 2026 — Centrus signed a $900M fixed-price contract with the US DOE to establish commercial-scale HALEU enrichment capacity at Piketon, with DOE options for up to an additional $170M, totalling ~$1.07B potential contract value. Q2 2026 earnings due August 11, 2026.

**Bull thesis:** Centrus holds a structural monopoly on HALEU in the Western world — no other licensed facility exists, no other company is close to qualification. Every US SMR project requires HALEU; with Oklo having an LOI for supply, and the US government explicitly mandating domestic fuel production, Centrus is the indispensable link in the nuclear revival supply chain. The $900M DOE contract is transformative: it funds the Piketon capacity expansion, de-risks the capital investment, and creates multi-year government-backed revenue visibility. If 2-3 SMR projects reach construction by 2028-2030, HALEU demand could exceed current Piketon capacity — triggering a pricing cycle analogous to uranium's in 2021-2024.

**Key catalysts ahead:** Q2 2026 earnings August 11 — first quarterly report since the $900M DOE contract; watch for contract milestone payment schedule, Piketon expansion timeline, and any new HALEU supply agreements with SMR developers. NRC approvals for SMR designs (each approval is a potential HALEU customer). Congressional appropriations for the DOE advanced reactor programme. Any new fuel contracts from SMR developers beyond the Oklo LOI.

**Main risks:** Revenue recognition is lumpy — the $900M contract uses milestone-based payments tied to construction progress at Piketon; any delay in construction or permitting pushes revenue out. Government-funding dependence: a shift in DOE priorities or a Congressional continuing resolution could slow payment. The legacy LEU segment is structurally declining as old enrichment contracts expire. Extreme price volatility (52-week range $144-$464) makes position sizing challenging.

**Competitive position:** Absolute monopoly on US HALEU production — no domestic competitor exists or is in the pipeline. The DOE's sole-source contract award reflects the absence of alternatives. Geopolitically excluded competitors (Russia's TENEX, which formerly supplied HALEU to US research reactors) make the domestic moat stronger.

**Rough valuation sense-check:** 52-week range $144-$464 — verify live before acting. At $164 (last snapshot, verify): market cap ~$490M vs FY2026 revenue guidance of $450-500M → P/S ~1x — extremely cheap for a monopoly in a strategically critical fuel supply chain. The risk is lumpiness of revenue; the reward is that SMR fuel demand is a genuine multi-decade secular story.

## VST — Vistra Energy

**What it does:** Vistra is a leading US independent power producer operating in deregulated electricity markets — primarily ERCOT (Texas) and PJM (Pennsylvania, Illinois, New Jersey, Ohio). Unlike regulated utilities with fixed returns, Vistra earns floating market prices for its electricity output. Its generation mix: nuclear (Comanche Peak, 2,300 MW in Texas), natural gas, and solar/storage. The company is acquiring Cogentrix Energy ($4B deal, ~5,500 MW of gas capacity, mid-to-late 2026 close) and has signed landmark AI-driven power purchase agreements: 20-year PPAs with Meta for 2,600+ MW of nuclear power (PJM facilities) and 20-year PPAs with AWS for 1,200 MW of carbon-free power from Comanche Peak.

**Latest results & financial health:** Q1 2026: revenue $5.6B (+43.4% YoY); adjusted EBITDA $1.5B (+20.5% YoY); net income $980M (major turnaround vs prior year). Q2 2026 earnings due August 7, 2026 (tomorrow from research date). Analyst consensus Q2 EPS: $2.43 (+140% YoY). Management guided $10B+ in cumulative cash generation through 2027. 16 of 17 analysts rate the stock Strong Buy; analyst consensus PT ~$230 (+41% upside from ~$163).

**Bull thesis:** Vistra is the most direct bet on power prices rising from AI data-centre demand in deregulated US markets. Unlike regulated peers with capped returns, Vistra captures full spot market upside when power prices spike — which they do in Texas summers and in PJM when AI data centres fill up. The Meta (2,600 MW) and AWS (1,200 MW) long-term nuclear PPAs de-risk a significant share of generation capacity at locked-in above-market rates, providing a pricing floor while retaining upside from uncontracted generation in tight power markets. Comanche Peak is a strategic irreplaceable asset — no new nuclear plants can be permitted and built in Texas in the next 15 years. The Cogentrix acquisition adds 5,500 MW of dispatchable gas to complement nuclear baseload, enabling Vistra to service both steady (nuclear PPA) and peaking (gas spot) demand from AI operators.

**Key catalysts ahead:** Q2 2026 earnings August 7 — consensus expects EPS $2.43 vs $1.01 prior year (+140%); any beat further validates the AI-power thesis. Cogentrix close (mid-to-late 2026) — adds capacity and future PPA optionality. PJM capacity market auction results — sets 2027-2028 forward power prices that directly affect Vistra's uncontracted generation revenue. Additional hyperscaler PPA announcements beyond Meta/AWS.

**Main risks:** PJM price cap proposals — US regulators are reviewing proposals to cap electricity prices in deregulated PJM markets, which would structurally limit Vistra's earnings upside from merchant power (the bear case for the stock). Deregulated market price volatility is symmetric — power prices can drop in mild weather or weak demand periods. ERCOT concentration (~40-50% of revenue in Texas exposes the company to weather and grid volatility. Cogentrix acquisition adds $4B of debt to a balance sheet already carrying leverage.

**Competitive position:** Top 3 US independent power producer. Nuclear assets are irreplaceable — Comanche Peak cannot be replicated. The direct competitors in nuclear-AI are Constellation Energy (CEG — pure-play nuclear, larger) and Talen Energy (TLN — Susquehanna plant co-located with AWS). Vistra's nuclear+gas hybrid model provides more dispatch flexibility than pure-nuclear peers.

**Rough valuation sense-check:** Market cap ~$46B at $163. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA likely $5-6B at Q1 run-rate + Cogentrix → EV/EBITDA ~8-10x. Forward P/E ~20-25x 2026 consensus EPS. Consensus PT $230 (+41% upside). Regulatory risk (PJM price caps) is the only structural bear case; if resolved favourably, re-rating toward $230 is credible.

## ENPH — Enphase Energy

**What it does:** Enphase is the leading US manufacturer of residential solar microinverters and home battery storage systems. A microinverter attaches to each individual solar panel and converts DC to AC at the panel level — enabling panel-level monitoring, better shade tolerance, and simpler system design vs string inverters (one box for the whole array). The IQ series microinverters, IQ Battery, and IQ EV charger constitute the core Enphase home energy stack. The company is also expanding into data-centre power with its IQ Solid-State Transformer (SST).

**Latest results & financial health:** Q2 2026: revenue $291.9M (-20% YoY but sequential improvement); non-GAAP EPS $0.46 (in-line). GAAP gross margin 60% — boosted by a $45.4M IEEPA tariff refund (one-time benefit). Non-GAAP gross margin 46.8%. Shipped 1.59M microinverters and 113.8 MWh of batteries. Q3 2026 guidance: $290-320M revenue, non-GAAP gross margin 44-47% (normalising for the tariff refund). Europe revenue +35% YoY, the bright spot. US resi solar permits industry-wide remain ~30% below prior year levels.

**Bull thesis:** Enphase is the premium quality brand in a market where the US resi solar recovery is a matter of when, not if. Interest rate sensitivity is real but directional — as rates stabilise or ease, installer confidence returns and permit volumes recover. The European market (which now represents a growing share of revenue) is growing faster than US and provides geographic diversification. The IQ Solid-State Transformer (SST) for data centres is a potentially disruptive new product leveraging Enphase's existing power electronics R&D — data centres need high-efficiency, reliable power conversion and Enphase has the engineering to deliver at data-centre spec. IQ 9 (GaN-based microinverter) improves efficiency and could expand Enphase's competitive moat. Chinese inverter ban (pending ruling) would remove a key US competitor.

**Key catalysts ahead:** Chinese inverter ban ruling — a key binary event for the whole US solar equipment sector; a ban would drive Enphase share gains in both resi and commercial solar. US resi solar permit data recovery — any month-over-month improvement would re-rate the sector. Q3 2026 results — the $290-320M guide implies sequential stabilisation; a beat signals the bottom is in. **Main risks:** US resi solar remains demand-challenged: high mortgage rates slow new home installs; the "solar IRA payback" narrative has dimmed with policy uncertainty. The Q2 $45.4M tariff refund inflated GAAP margins — normalised margins are 46-47% non-GAAP. Tesla Powerwall 3 is a genuine competitive threat in home batteries. SolarEdge, despite its own challenges, remains the main string-inverter alternative that installers can use to undercut Enphase on system cost. Revenue has been declining YoY for 6+ quarters — recovery requires a macro tailwind, not just execution.

**Competitive position:** #1 US resi solar microinverter market share by units. Premium-priced; installer loyalty is high because system reliability reduces callbacks. In home batteries, competes with Tesla Powerwall and SunPower. Internationally, Huawei and SMA compete in Europe; IRA provisions limit some Chinese product access in the US.

Historically Enphase traded at 40-80x earnings during the 2021-2022 boom; current valuation reflects the demand downturn. If revenue recovers to a $400-500M/quarter run-rate (plausible in 18-24 months), the stock could be a 2-3x from current levels.

## QS — QuantumScape

**What it does:** QuantumScape is developing a solid-state lithium-metal battery cell — replacing the liquid electrolyte in conventional Li-ion with a solid ceramic separator. The theoretical advantages: higher energy density (~70-80% more energy per kg vs today's best cells), faster charging, improved safety (no flammable electrolyte), and longer cycle life. The core product is the QSE-5 cell format. Manufacturing uses the Eagle Line (automated) and the Cobra fast separator production process, designed for eventual gigawatt-hour scale. The company is in the qualification/pilot stage — not yet in commercial production.

**Latest results & financial health:** Pre-revenue; in pilot production phase. Cash: $904M. Cash burn ~$290M/year → runway beyond 2028, sufficient to reach key OEM qualification milestones without equity dilution in the near term. Key 2026 milestone: June 2026 — Honda R&D signed a multi-year joint research agreement after completing a rigorous competitive benchmarking evaluation. VW remains the long-standing partner. QSE-5 cells are powering Ducati V21L race bikes — real-world validation in a high-performance application.

**Bull thesis:** QuantumScape has the most de-risked solid-state battery development program of any publicly traded company: two major OEMs (VW and Honda) have each independently evaluated and partnered with the technology after competitive reviews. Honda's conservatism as a supplier qualification partner makes its endorsement particularly credible — Honda does not enter multi-year R&D agreements lightly. The Eagle Line (automated) and Cobra (fast separator) production infrastructure addresses the "manufacturing at scale" problem that has historically killed solid-state programmes. At $904M cash and $5/share market price, the downside is partially cushioned by cash coverage (~$3/share in cash). If a single OEM production program is awarded, the stock could be a 10-20x from today's levels.

**Key catalysts ahead:** VW qualification decision for QSE-5 cells — the production timeline is 2026-2027; a positive verdict would be the largest re-rating event in QS history. Honda joint research programme advancing to a supply agreement or development partner arrangement. Eagle Line ramp to commercial sample quantities (yield and throughput data). Any energy density or cycle life publication or independent validation (academic or national lab) of the QSE-5 cell spec.

**Main risks:** Solid-state battery manufacturing at scale is an unsolved problem for the whole industry — every chemistry works in the lab but cost structures at volume remain unproven. The ceramic separator deposition at high-volume throughput is the central engineering challenge. VW's own in-house battery research and the broader slowing of EV demand could push OEM solid-state adoption timelines out. Cash burn of $290M/year means that if the Eagle Line hits technical snags, dilutive capital raises become necessary (current $904M covers 3+ years, but execution delays are common). Toyota is pursuing solid-state in-house and has deep manufacturing expertise.

**Competitive position:** Most advanced solid-state company in independent OEM qualification. Nearest public competitors: Solid Power (BMW/Ford backed), SLDP. Toyota, Samsung SDI, and LG Energy Solution have internal solid-state programmes. The key differentiator is QS's proprietary ceramic separator — others are pursuing polymer, oxide, or sulfide electrolytes with different trade-offs on conductivity, stability, and manufacturing.

**Rough valuation sense-check:** Market cap ~$1.4B at $5.17. Cash on hand ~$904M → enterprise value ~$500M. Valued entirely on technology option value. If 1-2 OEM programs are awarded, licensing/supply revenue could justify $10-30+/share. Current price reflects deep scepticism on the manufacturing-at-scale hurdle. Risk-reward is asymmetric for a small position sized as a moonshot.

## ASTS — AST SpaceMobile

**What it does:** AST SpaceMobile is building a broadband satellite constellation in LEO that delivers 4G/5G signals directly to unmodified standard smartphones — no special device or antenna required. The "direct-to-cell" architecture means remote coverage is possible anywhere on earth without ground infrastructure investment. Commercial partners include AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone, Rakuten, Bell Canada, TELUS, and 60+ other mobile network operators (MNOs) covering 3B+ subscribers globally. The BlueBird constellation is the commercial deployment phase; next-generation BlueBirds are among the largest communications satellites ever deployed in LEO.

**Latest results & financial health:** Q2 2026 earnings due August 10, 2026. Q1 2026 showed strong commercial progress: $1.2B+ in contracted revenue commitments; partnerships with 60+ MNOs covering 3B+ subscribers. Revenue is pre-material ($34M Q2 consensus estimate) — still ramping as satellites become operational. Key recent events: BlueBirds 8, 9, 10 launched successfully Q2 2026; BlueBirds 11, 12, 13 launched successfully August 5, 2026 — described as "the largest commercial communications arrays ever deployed in LEO," supporting peak data rates approaching 200 Mbps. Production advancing through satellite 42; BlueBirds 14-15-16 next in queue.

**Bull thesis:** If ASTS achieves its technical and commercial goals, it creates a fourth layer of global mobile infrastructure. MNO partners own the customer relationship and billing — they pay ASTS a wholesale rate per connected device. At even $5/month wholesale on 1% of the 3B addressable subscribers = ~$1.8B annual revenue; at 3% = ~$5.4B. The 60+ MNO partnerships are unprecedented for a company this early in development — carriers have signed because the addressable problem (covering rural, maritime, and remote areas) is worth billions in retained subscribers. The 200 Mbps capability of next-gen BlueBirds is a meaningful upgrade over first-generation (5-10 Mbps) — enabling video streaming and robust data use, not just SMS and low-bandwidth connectivity.

**Key catalysts ahead:** Q2 2026 earnings August 10 — first quarter with meaningful commercial satellite operational data; watch for active subscriber count, revenue per subscriber, and any raise in contracted-revenue guidance. BlueBird 11-13 commissioning into active service and initial throughput data. Subscriber activation reporting from AT&T and Verizon (US commercial service was launched late 2025). Next launch (BlueBirds 14-15-16) continuing the constellation build-out. FCC approval for additional frequency bands.

**Main risks:** Capital intensity: a full ~168-satellite constellation is likely $4-6B in total capex; the company has significant ongoing cash burn. SpaceX Starlink Direct-to-Cell (working with T-Mobile) is the primary competitor and is ahead in operational satellites. Each BlueBird satellite is very large — production cost and launch cost per satellite are high compared to smaller competing designs. The technology works in controlled testing but commercial throughput at millions of simultaneous connections at scale is unproven. Stock is extremely volatile (52-week range $36-$134); current ~$57 is 57% below the ATH.

**Competitive position:** One of two meaningful DTC providers — the other is SpaceX Starlink DTC. ASTS uses the MNO wholesale model (carrier as intermediary); SpaceX works directly with T-Mobile. The MNO partnership model could be more defensible long-term as carriers retain the customer relationship. No other company in the world has successfully commercialised broadband DTC to unmodified phones at scale — yet.

**Rough valuation sense-check:** Market cap ~$14B at $57. Q2 2026 revenue estimate $34M → implied annual run-rate ~$136M → P/S >100x on current revenue. Justified only if the subscriber ramp proves the model — at scale, $2-5B revenue run-rates are plausible but are 2-4 years away assuming execution. Treat as a high-conviction moonshot requiring a multi-year holding period.

## KTOS — Kratos Defense & Security Solutions

**What it does:** Kratos is a US defence company focused on affordable, attritable autonomous systems. Key products: XQ-58A Valkyrie (loyal wingman drone — an autonomous, reusable jet combat drone designed to operate alongside and collaborate with manned fighters); BQM-167 (high-speed aerial target drone for missile and weapons testing); HiCAT (high-speed strategic target for hypersonic weapons development); and satellite ground systems and electronic warfare products. "Attritable" means the platforms are priced to be expendable in combat if necessary — typically $2-5M for a Valkyrie vs $150M+ for an F-35.

**Latest results & financial health:** Q2 2026 (reported August 4, 2026): revenue $458.8M (+30.5% YoY from $351.5M). GAAP EPS $0.02 (flat, margins thin). Backlog: $2.084B. Proposal pipeline: $15B. Hypersonics business tracking to double from ~$200M (2025) to ~$400M (2026). Full-year 2026 organic revenue growth guidance raised to 19-23%. New win: ~$156M IDIQ contract from DOE/NNSA for mobile counter-UAS platforms (Project Solar Shield). Valkyrie production targets: 1.5 aircraft/month in 2027 (~35-40 units/year).

**Bull thesis:** Kratos is the purest play on the DoD's shift toward affordable autonomous combat systems. The Valkyrie thesis: the USAF's Collaborative Combat Aircraft (CCA) programme is seeking autonomous drones to operate alongside manned fighters in combat; these must be priced to be attritable (~$2-5M). Kratos is the only company currently in production at this specification with a flight-proven airframe. As "Golden Dome" multi-layered defence architecture gains DoD budget allocations, Kratos is positioned across all relevant capability gaps: Valkyrie for loyal-wingman offence, counter-UAS (Project Solar Shield) for defence, and HiCAT/BQM-167 hypersonic targets for testing the interceptors Golden Dome requires. Hypersonics revenue doubling to $400M in 2026 is the most visible near-term proof of the growth runway. The $15B proposal pipeline represents years of potential awards.

**Key catalysts ahead:** USAF CCA Phase 2 production contract (expected 2026-2027) — a Kratos win would be transformative and could move the stock significantly. Hypersonics revenue ramp confirmation (H2 2026 quarterly data). Golden Dome programme budget allocations from Congress — multiple Kratos systems map directly. Q3 2026 earnings — validation of 19-23% full-year growth guidance. Project Solar Shield $156M IDIQ delivery schedule.

**Main risks:** GAAP operating margins remain thin despite strong revenue growth — significant opex for Valkyrie production ramp, proposal development, and R&D means profitability is deferred. DoD budget continuing resolutions can delay contract awards unpredictably. CCA competition is effectively winner-take-most — if General Atomics or Shield AI captures the production contract, Kratos Valkyrie revenue potential narrows materially. Acquisition integration complexity across satellite, drone, and electronic warfare divisions. Stock has already run (+8% on the AVAV Q4 beat this week) — near-term some of the good news may be priced in.

**Competitive position:** Only company in production with a flight-proven affordable autonomous jet combat drone (Valkyrie). Unmatched in hypersonic target vehicles (BQM-167, HiCAT). Satellite ground systems compete with L3Harris, Northrop. The key moat is flight heritage — a drone that has physically flown 50+ missions is dramatically easier to win contracts for than a paper design.

**Rough valuation sense-check:** Market cap ~$6.5B at $51. FY2026 revenue at 19-23% growth on ~$1.56B 2025 base → ~$1.85-1.92B. P/S ~3.5x. Forward EV/EBITDA ~25-30x — not cheap but reasonable for a defence growth company with a $15B pipeline. A CCA production win could put KTOS in the $80-120 range; a loss keeps it range-bound.

## QUBT — Quantum Computing Inc.

**What it does:** Quantum Computing Inc. (QCi) develops photonic quantum computing and sensing systems. Its technology uses thin-film lithium niobate (TFLN) photonics — qubits encoded in light, operating at room temperature without the cryogenic dilution refrigerators required by superconducting quantum computers (IBM, Google, Rigetti). Commercial products include: the Entropy Quantum Computer (EQC) for optimization problems; the Reservoir Computer for machine learning; and the Quantum Photonic Vibrometer (QPV) for military/industrial sensing. The company significantly expanded via two acquisitions in late 2025/early 2026: Lumina Semiconductor Inc. (photonic chip fabrication) and NuCrypt LLC (quantum sensing/cryptography).

**Latest results & financial health:** Q1 2026: revenue $3.7M (vs $39K prior year — growth is acquisition-driven). Non-acquisition organic revenue ~$204K. Non-GAAP EPS loss $0.02, beating consensus by 60%. Cash: $1.4B (disproportionately large — from a significant 2025 equity raise). Contract backlog: $16M. ## ERAS — Erasca

**What it does:** Erasca is a clinical-stage precision oncology company with a singular focus: cancers driven by mutations in the RAS/MAPK signalling pathway. **Pipeline:**
- **ERAS-0015** (pan-RAS molecular glue, AURORAS-1 Phase 1): A mechanistically novel "molecular glue" that directly binds and inhibits RAS proteins at a site common to all RAS isoforms regardless of specific mutation — distinguishing it from mutation-specific inhibitors (sotorasib targets G12C only; adagrasib targets G12C). Positive preliminary Phase 1 monotherapy dose-escalation data announced in H1 2026: confirmed partial responses in multiple KRAS-mutant tumour types across dose levels, with a manageable safety and PK profile. Monotherapy expansion cohorts initiated Q2 2026 (US); combination dose-escalation cohorts (with a MEK inhibitor) initiated Q1 2026 (US), both ahead of prior guidance. Full Phase 1 monotherapy data expected H2 2026.
- **ERAS-4001** (pan-KRAS inhibitor, BOREALIS-1 Phase 1): IND cleared May 2025; Phase 1 trial underway; Phase 1 data expected H2 2026.
- **Naporafenib**: Pan-RAF inhibitor (licensed from Novartis); ongoing clinical evaluation within combination RAS/MAPK strategies.

**Financial health:** Pre-revenue, clinical-stage. Projected cash runway >3 years from most recent capital raise (funded through at minimum H2 2028). No meaningful debt. The dashboard notes "PRICE UNVERIFIED" — the stock price is uncertain and liquidity may be limited; verify before trading.

**Bull thesis:** KRAS-targeted therapy is one of the hottest sub-sectors in oncology investing: the first inhibitors proved the concept, but resistance develops rapidly (typically within 6-12 months on monotherapy). Pan-RAS or combination pathway approaches that deliver durable responses represent the next frontier. Erasca's molecular-glue mechanism (ERAS-0015) is fundamentally different from competitive-site KRAS inhibitors — it could have different efficacy and toxicity profiles. Early Phase 1 confirmed partial responses across multiple tumour types are encouraging, meaningful clinical signal in a pre-Phase-2 context.

**Key catalysts ahead:** ERAS-0015 full Phase 1 monotherapy data (H2 2026) — the single most important binary event for the company in the next 12 months. ERAS-4001 Phase 1 data (also H2 2026). Combination cohort results showing synergistic pathway suppression. Any business development (licensing/partnership) deal with large pharma interested in RAS pathway combinations.

**Main risks:** Classic binary biotech: both lead programmes are Phase 1. Even a positive Phase 1 result guarantees nothing about Phase 2/3 success. Competitive landscape is intensely contested — Mirati/BMS, Amgen, and most critically Revolution Medicines (RVMD, which has positive Phase 2 data for its RAS(ON) inhibitor, more advanced than Erasca's pipeline). Large pharma companies (Roche, AstraZeneca, J&J) have internal RAS pathway programmes with deep pockets. If Phase 1 data disappoint or show dose-limiting toxicity, the stock could fall 50%+. **Competitive position:** Most directly comparable to Revolution Medicines (RVMD) — the most advanced public company in pan-RAS inhibition, with Phase 2 data already showing strong signals in combination with SOS1 inhibition. Erasca's molecular-glue mechanism is mechanistically distinct from RVMD's competitive-binding approach and could have different clinical characteristics.

**Rough valuation sense-check:** Price unverified per dashboard. Typical clinical-stage oncology companies with this pipeline maturity and 3+ year runway trade at $200-700m market cap, but the number should be confirmed from a primary source before drawing conclusions.
